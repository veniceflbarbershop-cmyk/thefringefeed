**TL;DR:** If someone in the future builds a time machine, the past should be crawling with visitors. The most famous claimed visitor, John Titor, posted online in 2000 and 2001 and said he came from 2036. His predictions failed, and the trail led to a Florida entertainment lawyer. Stephen Hawking held a party for time travelers in 2009 and sent the invitations afterward. Nobody came. In 2013 two physicists searched the internet for people who knew things too early. They found nothing. That doesn't settle it, and the reasons why are worth thinking through.

## The obvious question

Physicists sometimes call it the tourist problem. If time travel to the past is ever invented, at any point in the entire future of humanity, then travelers should show up in our time. Over thousands or millions of years, somebody would want to see the moon landing, or the pyramids going up, or just check on great grandma.

So where are they? People have looked in three different ways.

## The soldier from 2036

On November 2, 2000, a user called TimeTravel_0 started posting on the Time Travel Institute forums. In January 2001 the same voice showed up on Art Bell's message board under a new name: John Titor.

Titor said he was an American soldier from the year 2036, sent back to 1975 to retrieve an IBM 5100 computer, a real and obscure machine he said was needed to fix a software problem in his time. He'd stopped in 2000 on the way home, he said, partly to see family. He described his time machine as mounted in a 1966 Chevrolet Corvette convertible, later a 1987 four wheel drive truck, and posted photos and diagrams. He talked about multiple timelines to explain why his presence wouldn't erase himself. Many of his stories were set in Florida, where he said he lived in the future.

He also made predictions, including a coming civil war in the United States and a nuclear conflict. In March 2001 he said goodbye and stopped posting for good.

## Following the paper trail

Two years after he vanished, a company called the John Titor Foundation published his posts as a book, *John Titor: A Time Traveler's Tale*. That gave investigators something real to follow.

Amateur researcher John Hughston, who ran a site called Hoax Hunter, traced the foundation to a P.O. box in Celebration, Florida, just outside Orlando. In 2009 he named its head as Larry Haber, a Florida entertainment lawyer. Haber's brother John Rick Haber was a computer scientist, and the Titor posts showed a specialist's knowledge of the IBM 5100. Hughston's theory was that one or both brothers created Titor. That theory has never been proven, and the Habers have not confirmed it.

The predictions did more damage than the investigation. There was no American civil war in the years Titor described. Titor also said the Large Hadron Collider would produce tiny black holes. According to skeptic Brian Dunning, every one of his predictions failed.

## A party with no guests

In late June 2009, Stephen Hawking threw a cocktail party at Gonville and Caius College in Cambridge, under a banner that said "Welcome Time Travellers." He sent out the invitations after the party was over, so only someone from the future could have known to come. He sat and waited. Nobody showed.

It was partly a stunt for a television program, and Hawking clearly enjoyed it. But it's a real experiment, cheap and clean. The invitation is still out there for anyone who can travel back to that afternoon.

## Searching the whole internet

In 2013, astrophysicist Robert Nemiroff of Michigan Technological University and graduate student Teresa Wilson ran a more systematic version. It started as a conversation over a poker game.

Their idea was to look for knowledge that showed up too early. They picked two terms that couldn't have existed before a certain date: "Pope Francis," a name nobody used before the March 2013 papal election, and "Comet ISON," discovered in late 2012. They searched for any posts mentioning either before those dates. They also checked whether anyone had searched for those terms too early.

The cleverest part came last. In September 2013 they asked time travelers to send a tweet or email using the hashtag #ICanChangeThePast2 or #ICannotChangeThePast2, dated on or before August 2013. A real time traveler could do it. Nobody else could.

They found nothing on any front. Nemiroff told NBC News that the result made time travel less likely in his mind. The work wasn't grant funded, and the paper is still on arXiv for anyone to read.

## The skeptic's case, at full strength

Put together, the evidence points one way. The most famous claimed traveler made specific predictions that failed and is linked to a business with a P.O. box. The two real experiments, one by the most famous physicist in the world and one designed to catch even a careless traveler, produced zero hits.

The tourist problem is hard to dodge. One hidden traveler is easy to imagine. A future with time machines would produce a constant stream of them over centuries, and every one would need perfect discipline to leave no trace. Someone would have slipped. Someone would have bought a lottery ticket.

## The believer's answer

There are honest replies, and a few of them come from the physics itself.

Some theoretical time machines, like those built from wormholes, could only reach back to the moment the machine was first built. If the first one is switched on in 2150, nobody can visit 2009, and Hawking's party was doomed from the start. Under the self-consistency idea from physics, a traveler might be unable to do anything that changes the record, like answering a hashtag that didn't get answered. And under the multiple timeline idea Titor himself described, a traveler who arrived in our past may have split off a different branch, leaving our records untouched.

Nemiroff himself acknowledged that the search doesn't rule out discreet travelers. Absence of evidence, as the saying goes, isn't evidence of absence.

As for Titor, his defenders argue that his arrival changed our timeline, which is exactly what he said would happen, so his predictions describe a world that branched away from ours.

## Your move

The searches were real. So are the loopholes. If time travelers are here, they're careful, or they can't reach us, or their past isn't our past. If they aren't, the universe may simply have closed that door. Check the paper, the forum archives, and Hawking's invitation, and decide which story fits.
